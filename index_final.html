Chapter 01 — Spatial Layouts & Geography

Where Does Culture Cluster?

Melbourne's inner suburbs are not a culinary monoculture. The CBD's dense Chinese and Japanese concentration, Carlton's iconic Italian corridor along Lygon Street, and the Vietnamese pockets of North Melbourne each reflect layers of migration history, community formation, and commercial identity..

Interactive Map

Melbourne Dining Venues by Cuisine Type, 2024

Most of the city's dining venues cluster in the CBD, Southbank, and Carlton, with smaller pockets in Fitzroy and North Melbourne. Cafes are the most dominant, to appeal to the younger demographic and the city's vibrant coffee culture, while Chinese and Japanese venues are heavily concentrated in the CBD. Scale Comparison

Venues vs. Total Seat Capacity by Suburb

Compared to the rest of the suburbs, Melbourne's CBD has the highest concentration of dining venues and total seat capacity. However, when we look at the average seating capacity per venue, Southbank stands out with a significantly higher average than the CBD, indicating that while it has fewer venues, those venues are larger on average. This is due to the concentration of larger establishments in Southbank, such as restaurants and bars with extensive seating.

Chapter 02 — Longitudinal Market Shifts, 2018–2024

Seven Years of Culinary Evolution

The 2020 pandemic left a visible scar across Melbourne's dining landscape, but recovery was uneven. Some cuisines bounced back with renewed intensity; others contracted permanently. The streamgraph captures this as a single flowing shape, while the line chart and bump chart reveal which suburbs and cuisines led the charge back to the table.

Streamgraph

The Flow of Melbourne's Culinary Scene, 2018–2024

The streamgraph shows melbournes venues growth and decline over 2018 to 2024. The narrow 2020 waist across all layers captures the pandemic's sudden, total disruption followed by the broad re-expansion that followed.

Regional Trends

Establishment Growth by Suburb, 2018–2024

The CBD dominates in absolute terms, but were hit the hardest by the pandemic, compared to the other suburbs. Compared to the CBD, other suburbs had a more moderate decline in 2020 and a steadier recovery. Hover over any line to highlight a suburb's trajectory.

Bump Chart

Cuisine Popularity Rankings, 2018–2024

Korean cuisine climbed the rankings consistently across all seven years. Cafes maintained their top position throughout, with Japanese and Chinese venues not far behind. There is some volatility in the rankings, but overall trends remain stable.

Chapter 03 — Dual-Source Demographic Correlations

Does Heritage Shape What We Eat?

Does a suburb's cultural makeup predict which restaurants thrive there? Carlton's Italian heritage clearly supports its restaurant density, the CBD's large Chinese-ancestry population coincides with its cuisine dominance; yet some cultures appear to under-index commercially, suggesting gaps in representation.

Correlation Scatter

Italian Ancestry vs. Italian Restaurant Count, 2021

As shown in the graph, Carlton with over 10% of the population with Italian ancestry has the highest concentration of Italian establishments behind the CBD, due to the effects of tourism which drives demand for authentic Italian cuisine.

Cultural Matrix Heatmap

Suburb × Cuisine Concentration, 2021

The CBD's dominance spans every category, due to its central location and diverse population.

Dumbbell Plot

Where Cultures Over- & Under-Index in Melbourne's Dining Scene

For four different cultural groups, each row compares a suburb's population ancestry share (hollow circle) with that cuisine's share of total restaurants (solid circle). A solid circle to the right of the hollow circle means the cuisine is commercially over-represented relative to the local population. A solid circle to the left means it lags behind population share — suggesting either lower commercial density or higher dining-out rates for that community.

Chapter 04 — Seating Layout & Infrastructure Capacity

The Spaces We Share

The pandemic permanently rewired how Melbourne restaurants deploy their physical space. Outdoor seating — once a nice-to-have for warm summer evenings — became existential during lockdowns, and council policy moved to accommodate it. The charts below trace the remarkable shift toward alfresco dining, showing which suburbs and cuisine types have most aggressively embraced open-air infrastructure and what the city-wide split looks like in 2024.

Area Chart

Indoor vs. Outdoor Seat Growth, 2018–2024

There was a decline in indoor seating capacity due to the pandemic in 2019 and has stabilised after 2023

Donut Chart

City-Wide Seating Mix, 2024

Nowadays, around 20% of Melbourne's restaurant seating is outdoors, which was a significant shift from pre-pandemic norms.

100% Stacked Bar

Indoor vs. Outdoor Seating Mix by Suburb, 2024

West Melbourne and Melbourne CBD show the highest indoor seating proportions, suggesting a stronger reliance on enclosed dining spaces in dense commercial areas. In contrast, suburbs such as Parkville, Port Melbourne, and Melbourne (Remainder) have comparatively larger outdoor seating shares, indicating greater use of alfresco dining environments. Middle-range suburbs including Carlton, Docklands, and Southbank display a more balanced seating mix, though indoor seating still remains the majority.

Chapter 05 — Consumer Sentiment & Quality

What Do Diners Actually Think?

TripAdvisor rankings for Melbourne's top 500 restaurants reveal how pricing and customer satisfaction intersect across the city’s dining scene.Two key trends emerge from Melbourne’s restaurant scene: highly reviewed venues are not always the highest rated, while many lesser-known restaurants achieve perfect scores with fewer reviews. Additionally, expensive dining does not guarantee better experiences, as lower-priced venues often receive stronger average ratings.
